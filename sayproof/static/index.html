How it works

  1. Your words become text. A recording is transcribed first. Every claim keeps its time in the recording, so you can jump to the moment you said it.

  2. Each claim is looked up. Reference sources first: public bodies, science organisations, encyclopedias, major news outlets. The open web only if they don't settle it, and the result says so.

  3. You get a verdict and a fix. A wrong claim comes with a sourced line to say instead. Claims about your own company are never searched; Sayproof asks for your data.

What the verdicts mean

Supported
The sources confirm it.
Contradicted
The sources show it's false. You get a sourced line to say instead.
Outdated
True once, not today, like “Pluto is our ninth planet”. A claim that names its year is judged for that year.
Misleading
The facts are right, but the picture your audience takes away is wrong, like “the edge of the universe” for the observable universe.
Can't verify
The sources don't settle it, or it's your own data. Sayproof asks for your source instead of guessing.

No source, no verdict. Every verdict except “can't verify” quotes a sentence that appears word for word in the source. If it doesn't, the verdict drops to “can't verify”.

Written by AI, checked against sources

Claims and verdicts come from an AI model, NVIDIA Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory, working from Tavily search results. It can be wrong. Open the quoted source before you change your talk.

What happens to your recording

Online, your recording is stored in a private storage bucket and transcribed by ElevenLabs; the transcript is saved with it. Neither is public. Don't upload anything confidential. To keep everything on your own computer, run the local version, where NVIDIA Parakeet transcribes on your machine.
